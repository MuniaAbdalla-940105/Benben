## What changed from round 1
Round 1 explored one idea three ways: a capstone with steps or a tower. Round 2 uses eight different territories. None of them reuses the capstone-with-tower form. Each concept is two or three flat shapes.

## The eight concepts
Scores are out of 5: Originality / Small size (16 px) / Premium feel / Brief fit. Brief fit means it links the Benben, real estate and auctions, and avoids a house, a gavel and a tourist pyramid.

### Story and risk for each concept
1. **Bennu Rising.** In the Heliopolis myth the Bennu heron rose from the Benben at creation and returns to renew it, standing for rebirth and rising value. The wings are cut at 51.84°. *Risk:* it can read as a letter W, a generic eagle or military insignia, or a dragonfly. At 16 px it becomes a "W".
2. **First Light.** The Benben was the first ground to catch the sun. The horizon is land, and its single step is the bid. *Risk:* a sunset over stairs, similar to travel, resort and solar logos. It holds at 16 px.
3. **B Spire.** B for BEN-BEN. The bowls read as floors, and the gold spire is the capstone on a modern tower. *Risk:* the spire can look like a pencil tip or an accent mark. **The counters fail at 16 px**, where it becomes a plain b.
4. **Ba Nuqta.** ب begins بن بن, بيت (home) and بيع (sale). The rhombic nuqta is the calligrapher's unit of proportion, much as the seked was for the pyramid builders. It is Egyptian and Arab with no Pharaonic image. *Risk:* Latin readers may see a tray, a shelf or a bracket. There is no rising idea in the mark itself.
5. **The Opening.** The opening is the way into property, and the tower inside it is the asset you win. *Risk:* a pointed doorway reads as a church or mosque window, or a castle door. **It is too close to the "house" family**, so I scored it lowest on fit.
6. **Golden Hour.** Every auction lasts 24 hours, so the auction is time. The wedge is both the last hour on the dial and a capstone seen from above. *Risk:* a pie chart, timer, target or map pin. It holds at 16 px, but the 76.3° story needs telling.
7. **First Ground.** The Benben is the primordial mound that rose from the waters of Nun, and property begins with land. *Risk:* a sunset, cloud, loaf of bread or hamburger. **It fails at 16 px**: the water lines merge.
8. **The Hyphen.** BEN-BEN is two words joined by a hyphen. Here they become the seller's building and the buyer's taller one, and the gold step between them is the deal. It ties straight to the gold hyphen in the round 1 wordmark. *Risk:* a bar chart or stats icon. I also tried a raised bid paddle and dropped it, because it read as a lollipop or magnifier at 16 px.

## Small-size results (all viewed at 60, 29 and 16 px)
- **Hold at 16 px:** 2 First Light, 4 Ba Nuqta (the best), 5 The Opening, 6 Golden Hour, 8 The Hyphen.
- **Borderline:** 1 Bennu Rising (it holds, but reads as "W").
- **Fail:** 3 B Spire (the counters close), 7 First Ground (the water lines merge).

## Shortlist (top 3, not refined)
1. **Ba Nuqta (4).** This is the most ownable mark and the best at 16 px. It is the only concept that is clearly Egyptian and Arab without Pharaonic imagery, and the letter links the name, home and sale. Trade-off: the auction and rising story must come from the brand system (the gold hyphen in the wordmark, the LIVE moments), not from the mark.
2. **Golden Hour (6).** This is the strongest auction idea, because the 24-hour auction is the product. The 76.3° wedge hides the pyramid where only the curious will find it, which keeps it far from tourist imagery. It also gives the app a countdown motif it can own.
3. **The Hyphen (8).** It comes from the name itself, so the mark and the wordmark become one system. It shows buyer, seller and the rising deal in three flat shapes. Trade-off: the bar-chart look.

Possible combinations if the stakeholder likes two of them: Ba Nuqta with a gold dot that is the hyphen step (4 + 8), or a Golden Hour dial used as the app's countdown motif next to any chosen mark.
